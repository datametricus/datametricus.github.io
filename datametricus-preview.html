How we work

Every deliverable is structured so that results can be independently verified. Code is version-controlled and documented. Assumptions are stated explicitly and tested against alternatives. Data provenance is recorded at every transformation step.

We do not produce black-box outputs. Clients receive not only findings but the documented analytical chain that produced them — enabling internal review, regulatory examination, or downstream use by their own teams.

All outputs carry a documented audit trail from raw input to final result.

Working principles

Reproducibility by default

No manual steps without documentation. Pipelines are scripted, not assembled by hand.

Explicit assumptions

Sensitivity analyses accompany every primary estimate. Uncertainty is quantified, not suppressed.

Independence preserved

Analytical conclusions are not adjusted for client preference. Findings are reported as the data supports.

What we need from you

The following reduces time to proposal and improves the precision of the engagement specification.

  1. Data access: Format, volume, access method, and any governance constraints on the data to be analysed.
  2. Analytical objective: The specific question to be answered, the decision it informs, and the audience for the output.
  3. Timeline: Deadlines that are fixed (regulatory, publication, board) versus those that are flexible.
  4. Constraints: Software environment, output format requirements, confidentiality restrictions.
  5. Decision-maker: Who will use the output and at what level of technical fluency.

Frequently asked questions

Do you work with proprietary or sensitive data?

Yes. Data handling arrangements are specified in the engagement agreement before any data is shared. We do not retain client data beyond the agreed project period.

Can outputs be used in regulatory submissions?

Analytical outputs are structured and documented to a standard appropriate for regulatory review. Responsibility for submission rests with the client; we provide the documented methodology and code necessary to support examination.

What software tools do you use?

Primary tools are R, Python, Quarto, and Git. We do not use proprietary analytical platforms that limit client access to the underlying code.

How do you handle disagreements about findings?

Analytical conclusions are determined by the data and the stated methodology. We do not revise conclusions to reflect client preference.

What is the typical turnaround for a scoping proposal?

A scoping call followed by a written proposal typically takes five to seven working days, provided the required information is available at first contact.

Training

Structured programmes for analysts who need to work at higher levels of rigour, reproducibility, and methodological transparency.

Three programme tracks

Each track is self-contained and can be taken independently or in sequence.

Track 1

Foundations of Quantitative Analysis

Audience: Early-career analysts Format: 2-day workshop Duration: 14 hours

Core statistical concepts applied to real data, with emphasis on understanding what methods require and what they cannot determine.

  • Probability, uncertainty, and interval estimation
  • Hypothesis testing: what it does and does not establish
  • Regression foundations and assumption checking
  • Communicating quantitative findings
  • Introduction to R and reproducible scripts
Track 2

Applied Modelling

Audience: Analysts with statistical foundations Format: 3-day workshop Duration: 18 hours

Practical model construction in health, insurance, or policy contexts. Covers model selection, validation, and governance documentation.

  • Generalised linear models and extensions
  • Survival analysis and time-to-event data
  • Mortality and morbidity modelling fundamentals
  • Model validation and out-of-sample testing
  • Structuring technical assumptions papers
Track 3

Reproducible Workflows

Audience: Analysts and research teams Format: 2-day workshop or in-house Duration: 12 hours

Practical implementation of reproducible, version-controlled pipelines using R, Python, Quarto, and Git.

  • Version control with Git
  • Quarto for reproducible reports and documents
  • Pipeline structure: data contracts, transformation, output
  • Automated output checking and validation routines
  • Team workflows: branching, review, and release

Who attends

Participants are typically working analysts and researchers in insurance, reinsurance, public health, or regulated bodies who require stronger statistical foundations, more rigorous modelling skills, or the ability to produce reproducible outputs that withstand external scrutiny.

A substantial proportion come from organisations that have identified a gap between the analytical quality they need to produce and the methods currently in use. Others attend to standardise practice across a team or to support a project with elevated evidence requirements.

No prior programming experience is assumed for Track 1. Tracks 2 and 3 assume working familiarity with at least one analytical environment.

About

Independent quantitative advisory. Not a technology vendor. Not a generalist consultancy.

What DataMetricus is

DataMetricus is an independent quantitative advisory providing statistical and actuarial modelling, applied health metrics, and reproducible research infrastructure to institutional clients. Engagements are analytical in nature: the deliverable is documented, auditable work, not software.

The practice operates at the intersection of statistical rigour, methodological transparency, and institutional accountability — areas where analytical quality has direct consequences for governance, policy, or financial decision-making.

Independence is a defining feature of the practice. Findings are not adjusted for client preference. Methods are documented to a standard that allows independent verification.

What DataMetricus is not

We are not a software development company. We do not build products, platforms, or dashboards as primary deliverables.

We are not a generalist management consultancy. We do not provide strategy, organisational change, or process design services.

We are not a staffing agency. We do not place contractors or provide body-shop analytical resource. All work is delivered directly by DataMetricus.

Principal

DataMetricus was founded by a quantitative analyst with background in actuarial practice, applied epidemiology, and reproducible research methods. Work spans the public and private sector, with experience in insurance, public health institutions, and regulated bodies in the UK and Europe.

Prior roles have included statistical analysis for national health surveillance programmes, actuarial modelling for life and health insurers, and methods development for academic research groups. Teaching and training have been a consistent part of the practice throughout.

Working principles

Stated as operational commitments, not aspirational slogans.

Reproducibility is a requirement, not a preference

Every analysis we deliver can be re-run from documented inputs. No manual steps are permitted without documentation.

Uncertainty is reported, not suppressed

Primary estimates are always accompanied by a characterisation of uncertainty. Sensitivity analyses are conducted as a matter of method.

Independence is maintained throughout

Analytical findings are determined by the data and the methodology. Client relationships do not alter the direction of reported results.

Scope is kept within competence

We decline engagements that fall outside our documented area of expertise. Referrals to appropriate specialists are offered when we are not the right fit.
